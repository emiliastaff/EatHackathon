What each archetype cares about

From a product-head interview. Every one of the 2,048 on/off combinations of these 11 drivers maps to its nearest archetype in the full dataset.

How a score is made

signal(product, driver) = 0.6 × listing claims it + 0.4 × min(1, 3 × share of reviews mentioning it)
fit(product, archetype) = mean of signals over the drivers that archetype cares about
store fit = Σ shopper mix(archetype) × fit(product, archetype)
gap(driver) = importance in this store × (1 − signal) → ranked recommendations

Data used

    Assumptions and limits

    • The 3 archetypes come from a product-head interview. They are hypotheses, not measured segments.
    • Store shopper mixes are starting values. Adjust them on the deep-dive.
    • Signals come from keyword rules on listings and reviews; design and coolness are only partly visible in text.
    • Reviews show who bought, not who drank it. Gifting and household buying blur shopper and consumer.

    Making it real

    • Data ownership: swap scraped reviews for consented Watch Humans video reviews, which capture what nearly won and why.
    • Privacy: no reviewer names or IDs are stored; brands see aggregates and anonymised quotes only.
    • Calibration: fit the archetype weights and store mixes to sell-through and loyalty data from a retail partner, then test predictions on the next range review.
    • Cost and scale: tagging runs at pennies per thousand reviews; the same pipeline works for any category with reviews.
